# The Power of Whole Grains

Whole grains keep all three parts of the kernel — the bran, germ, and endosperm — instead of being stripped down like refined grains. That's what makes them so nourishing: they're rich in fiber, protein, vitamins, and minerals that help you feel full, support steady digestion, and are linked to lower risk of heart disease and diabetes.

## A Quick Prep Tip

Grains, beans, and nuts naturally contain phytic acid, which can make it a little harder for your body to absorb minerals like iron and calcium. The fix is simple and something cultures have done for generations: **soak your grains, beans, and nuts before cooking** (a few hours to overnight), and cook them well rather than eating them raw. This naturally reduces phytic acid and makes the nutrients more available to your body. Pairing grain-based meals with vitamin-C-rich foods (citrus, bell peppers, broccoli) also helps your body absorb iron better.

## Whole Grains to Explore

- **Brown rice** — fiber, magnesium, selenium; an easy swap for white rice
- **Quinoa** — high in protein and fiber, with iron, magnesium, and zinc; great as a salad or rice/pasta substitute
- **Oats** — rich in soluble fiber that supports healthy cholesterol; classic for breakfast or baking
- **Barley** — fiber (especially beta-glucan) for cholesterol support; wonderful in soups and stews
- **Whole wheat** — fiber, protein, iron, and B vitamins; choose whole wheat bread, pasta, and flour
- **Millet** — gluten-free, rich in magnesium and antioxidants; good as a side or in porridge
- **Farro** — fiber, protein, magnesium, and zinc; excellent in salads and risottos
- **Amaranth** — gluten-free, high protein and fiber; great as porridge or in baking
- **Teff** — gluten-free, rich in calcium, iron, and magnesium; wonderful in pancakes and flatbreads
- **Buckwheat** — gluten-free, rich in protein and antioxidants; classic for pancakes and noodles

## Easy Ways to Add Them In

- Swap refined grains for whole grain versions in bread, pasta, and tortillas
- Build hearty grain salads with vegetables, herbs, and beans or tofu
- Start the day with oatmeal, whole grain toast, or a quinoa breakfast bowl
- Stir grains like barley or brown rice into soups and stews for texture and staying power
- Use whole grain wraps, pizza dough, or breadcrumbs in place of refined versions
