# Salt: What Actually Matters

Sodium is essential — your body needs it for nerve function, fluid balance, and muscle contraction. The real issue isn't salt itself, it's how much of it most people consume, largely from processed and packaged foods rather than the salt shaker.

## Why Moderation Matters

Consistently high sodium intake is linked to higher blood pressure, fluid retention, and increased cardiovascular risk over time — which is why health organizations like the WHO and American Heart Association recommend keeping daily intake around 2,300 mg or less. The biggest culprit is usually hidden sodium in processed and packaged foods, not what you add at the table.

## Table Salt vs. Other Salts

**Table salt** is refined to remove impurities and typically includes iodine — an essential nutrient for thyroid health that's genuinely hard to get enough of otherwise.

**Himalayan pink salt and Celtic sea salt** are less processed and contain trace minerals (calcium, magnesium, potassium, and others) that give them their color and flavor. These are real, but the amounts are small enough that they don't meaningfully change your nutrition — think of the appeal as flavor and minimal processing, not a mineral supplement. One practical note: these salts usually don't contain iodine, so if you use them as your primary salt, make sure you're getting iodine elsewhere.

## Getting Enough Iodine

If you're not using iodized table salt regularly, seaweed is an excellent natural source — kelp, wakame, nori, and dulse are all iodine-rich and easy to add to soups, salads, or as seasoning.

## Flavor Without the Extra Sodium

- **Herbs and spices** — basil, thyme, oregano, cumin, turmeric, and paprika add real depth without any sodium, plus some offer antioxidant benefits of their own
- **Citrus** — a squeeze of lemon or lime brightens a dish the way salt often does
- **Aromatics** — fresh or powdered garlic and onion add richness without leaning on salt
- **Salt-free seasoning blends** — a simple way to season generously without the sodium

*As always, if you have specific health conditions affecting sodium needs, it's worth checking with your doctor or dietitian for personalized guidance.*
