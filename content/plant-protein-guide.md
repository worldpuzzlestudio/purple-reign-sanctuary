# Getting Enough Protein on a Plant-Based Diet

One of the most common worries about eating plant-based is protein — but it's easier to meet your needs than most people expect. A single cup of cooked lentils delivers about 18 grams of protein, alongside fiber and nutrients that come standard with plant foods and aren't found in animal protein sources.

## How Much Protein Do You Actually Need?

The general daily recommendation is:
- **Adult men:** about 56 grams
- **Adult women:** about 46 grams

(Individual needs shift with activity level, muscle mass, and overall health — think of these as a solid baseline.)

## Protein-Rich Plant Foods

- Cooked lentils (1 cup): ~18g
- Dry red beans (½ cup): ~21g
- Dry black beans (½ cup): ~24g
- Chia seeds (½ cup): ~18g
- Flaxseeds (½ cup): ~18g

## Complete vs. Incomplete Proteins

Your body needs 9 essential amino acids from food — it can't make them on its own. A "complete protein" contains all 9; an "incomplete protein" is missing one or more, but combining foods across the day easily fills the gap (rice + beans is the classic example).

**Complete plant protein sources:**
- Quinoa — 8g per cup, cooked
- Tofu — 8–10g per 100g
- Tempeh — 18g per 100g
- Buckwheat — 23g per 100g, raw
- Soybeans — 36g per 100g, raw
- Hemp seeds — 11g per 30g
- Chia seeds — 4g per 2 tbsp
- Spirulina — 4g per tbsp

**Incomplete sources (still valuable — just pair with variety):**
- Grains — ~5g per 100g cooked
- Nuts and seeds — 6–9g per 30g
- Legumes and beans — 7–9g per 100g
- Vegetables — green peas ~7g/cup; spinach and broccoli ~3–4g/cup

Don't stress about hitting "complete" protein at every single meal — eating a variety of plant proteins across your day covers your amino acid needs just fine.

## A Sample Day: ~73 Grams of Protein

**Breakfast — Tofu Scramble (29g)**
Firm tofu, spinach, bell peppers, and onion, seasoned with turmeric and garlic, served with whole-grain toast and sliced avocado.

**Lunch — Quinoa Salad (22g)**
Cooked quinoa, chickpeas, cucumber, cherry tomatoes, parsley, and chopped almonds, dressed with olive oil and lemon.

**Dinner — Lentil and Vegetable Stir-Fry (22g)**
Cooked lentils, mixed vegetables, and brown rice, tossed in a garlic-ginger sauce.

## Easy Combos for Complete Protein

- Brown rice and beans
- Sprouted-grain bread (like Ezekiel bread — easier to digest thanks to sprouting, though it still contains gluten from wheat, barley, and spelt)
- A smoothie with spirulina and nuts
- Salads combining beans, nuts, and whole grains
- Soups with beans, vegetables, and whole grains
- Grain bowls layering grains, vegetables, beans or hummus, nuts, and hemp seeds
