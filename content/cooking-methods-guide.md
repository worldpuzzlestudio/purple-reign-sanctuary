# Cooking Methods for Plant-Based Meals

Different techniques bring out different flavors and textures — here's a quick guide to the essentials.

## Sautéing
Quick cooking in a small amount of oil over medium-high heat, great for developing flavor and light caramelization on vegetables.
- Heat a skillet, add a small amount of oil (extra virgin olive oil and cold-pressed coconut oil are great options)
- Add sliced vegetables in a single layer
- Stir frequently, cook 3–5 minutes until tender
- Finish with fresh herbs and salt to taste

## Roasting
Brings out natural sweetness in vegetables (and even fruit) through dry oven heat.
- Preheat oven to 400°F (200°C)
- Cut vegetables into even pieces, toss with oil, spread on a lined baking sheet in a single layer
- Roast 20–30 minutes, flipping halfway, until golden and tender
- Finish with herbs and salt

## Steaming
A gentle method that preserves nutrients, color, and natural flavor — ideal for delicate vegetables.
- Bring an inch of water to a boil in a pot
- Set a steamer basket above the water (not touching it)
- Add vegetables, cover, and steam 5–7 minutes until tender-crisp
- Finish with herbs and salt

## Baking
Dry oven heat, perfect for casseroles, bread, muffins, and veggie-based mains.
- Preheat oven to 300–375°F (150–190°C)
- Layer ingredients (like cubed tofu and vegetables) in a baking dish
- Drizzle with oil, season with herbs and salt
- Cover and bake 25–30 minutes until tender and golden

## Stir-Frying
Fast, high-heat cooking that keeps vegetables crisp and nutrient-rich — traditionally done in a wok.
- Heat oil in a hot pan or wok
- Add garlic and ginger briefly until fragrant
- Cook protein (like tofu) undisturbed until golden, then flip
- Push aside, add vegetables, and stir-fry until crisp-tender
- Toss with your favorite sauce and serve over rice or noodles

## Boiling
Simple and reliable for grains, pasta, legumes, and vegetables.
- Bring a full pot of water to a rolling boil
- Add ingredients, ensuring they're fully submerged
- Cook until tender, checking periodically to avoid overcooking
- Drain and serve hot or cold

**A note on ingredients:** any neutral or flavorful cooking oil works well across these methods — extra virgin olive oil and coconut oil are popular whole-food choices, but there's no need to feel locked into one type. Good unrefined options include avocado oil (light sautéing), and flax, walnut, or hemp oil (best drizzled cold over finished dishes — heat breaks down their delicate fats). Unrefined oils are actually the *least* processed choice — just pressed, nothing added or stripped — though that also means a lower smoke point, so save them for gentler cooking rather than high-heat frying. The same "keep it simple" approach goes for salt — sea salt, Himalayan, or regular table salt all work fine; specialty salts mainly offer subtle flavor differences.
