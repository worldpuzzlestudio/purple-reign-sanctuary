# Beans and Peas: Small but Mighty

Legumes have fed people well for thousands of years, and for good reason — beans and peas are some of the most protein- and fiber-rich foods in the plant world, plus a great source of folate, iron, magnesium, potassium, and B vitamins. They're a staple for anyone eating plant-based, and easy to build a meal around.

## A Few Favorites to Know

- **Green peas** — sweet and tender, great raw or cooked in soups, salads, and stir-fries
- **Split peas** — dried and halved, perfect for hearty soups, stews, and dal
- **Snow peas** — crisp, edible pods with sweet peas inside; a stir-fry and salad favorite
- **Black beans** — dense and earthy, wonderful in soups, stews, and rice bowls
- **Kidney beans** — creamy with a hint of sweetness, classic in chili, salads, and dips
- **Chickpeas** — firm and nutty, the base of hummus, falafel, and curries

## A Quick Prep Tip

Like grains, beans and peas contain phytic acid, which can make it a bit harder for the body to absorb minerals. Soaking is the easy fix:

1. Rinse beans or peas well.
2. Cover with plenty of water — a splash of lemon juice helps too.
3. Soak overnight (12–24 hours is ideal).
4. Drain, rinse again, and cook as usual.

Sprouting takes it a step further (rinse and drain twice a day for a few days until they sprout) and boosts nutrition even more, but soaking alone makes a real difference. Cooking beans and peas thoroughly — simmering or pressure cooking — also helps.

**Two more easy wins:**
- Pair beans with vitamin-C-rich foods like tomatoes, peppers, or broccoli to help your body absorb their nutrients better.
- New to eating a lot of beans? Start with smaller portions and build up gradually — your digestion adjusts over time.
