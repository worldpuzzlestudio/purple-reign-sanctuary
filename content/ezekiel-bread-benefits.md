# A Recipe as Old as Scripture

Long before "whole grain" was a nutrition buzzword, Ezekiel 4:9 described a bread made from a mix of wheat, barley, beans, lentils, millet, and spelt — grains and legumes combined into one loaf. It's a beautiful picture of what modern nutrition has only caught up to: whole grains and legumes together are one of the most nourishing combinations you can eat.

## Why This Combination Works

**Complete nourishment.** Grains and beans complement each other's amino acids, so together they offer a fuller protein profile than either provides alone — a principle whole-food, plant-based eating has leaned on for generations, long before "complete protein" was a term.

**Steady energy.** The fiber in both beans and whole grains slows digestion, helping you avoid the energy crashes that come with refined, low-fiber foods.

**Gut support.** Beans and whole grains are some of the richest everyday sources of fiber, which feeds the good bacteria in your gut — a foundation for digestion and immunity alike.

**Heart health.** Replacing refined grains with whole grains, and adding beans regularly, is consistently linked to healthier cholesterol levels and reduced heart disease risk.

*Whether you bake your own version of Ezekiel bread or simply add more beans and whole grains to your week, you're following a pattern of nourishment that's stood the test of thousands of years.*
